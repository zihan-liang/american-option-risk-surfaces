\section{Purpose}
This report consolidates the project evidence into manuscript-safe claims. The project contribution is a reproducible research-engineering workflow: a validated CN/PSOR benchmark first, audited classical risk-surface experiments second, and separate neural diagnostic components only after validated PSOR labels exist.

\section{Method Summary}
The numerical source of truth is a Crank--Nicolson finite-difference method with PSOR projection for the American option variational inequality. All later labels for prices, early-exercise boundaries, Delta, and Gamma come from this audited benchmark. Neural components are modular: a positive continuation-premium price surrogate, a direct boundary diagnostic head, and a bounded supervised Delta head.

\section{Allowed Claims}
The project may claim that a validated CN/PSOR benchmark generates audited American put and dividend-paying American call risk surfaces within tested regimes. It may also claim that the positive continuation-premium surrogate enforces $V\geq\Phi$ by construction for price/premium prediction, that direct boundary heads provide scoped supervised diagnostics, and that a bounded supervised Delta head provides strict-mask Delta diagnostics.

The integrated workflow may be described as scenario screening with PSOR audit. This wording is important because PSOR remains the benchmark, label generator, and audit mechanism for new regimes.

\section{Restricted Claims}
The project must not claim that a single network solves price, boundary, and Greeks. The price surrogate is not a Greek model. The boundary head is not an exact free-boundary solver. The Delta head is not a Gamma model. The workflow does not replace PSOR. The grid-240 reference used in the engineering audit is not an analytic reference.

\section{Limitations}
The strongest limitations are numerical and statistical. First, the solver evidence is engineering-scale and should not be described as final publication-grade accuracy across all regimes. Second, Gamma is delicate near payoff kinks and free boundaries, so strict masks and caution remain necessary. Third, boundary estimation is weaker than price and Delta estimation, especially for call-only generalization. Fourth, the integrated workflow is evaluated on six deterministic held-out PSOR cases, not on an exhaustive production envelope.

\section{Reproducibility}
The final claim audit is generated by \texttt{experiments/14\_claim\_audit.py} with \texttt{configs/claim\_audit.yaml}. The main output is \texttt{results/tables/final\_claim\_matrix.csv}, supported by \texttt{forbidden\_language\_scan.csv}, \texttt{missing\_artifacts.csv}, and \texttt{claim\_audit\_summary.json}.

\section{Conclusion}
The project is ready to be reported as a reproducible computational-finance workflow with careful claim boundaries: validated classical benchmark, audited risk surfaces, obstacle-consistent price/premium surrogate, scoped boundary diagnostics, bounded Delta diagnostics, and integrated scenario screening with PSOR audit.
