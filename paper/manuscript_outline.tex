\section{Introduction}
American option risk surfaces, Greeks, and early-exercise boundaries. Position the project as a reproducible research-engineering study: a validated classical benchmark first, then diagnostic-aware neural surrogate components. The contribution is computational and methodological rather than a new financial-mathematics theorem.

\section{Literature Review}
Classical American option theory motivates the variational inequality, obstacle, and free-boundary formulation used throughout the project \cite{JailletLambertonLapeyre1990,HuangPang1998}. Early-exercise premium and boundary characterizations motivate treating the continuation premium and the free boundary as meaningful objects rather than incidental plots \cite{Kim1990,CarrJarrowMyneni1992,Peskir2005,ChenChadam2007,EvansKuskeKeller2002}.

Finite-difference studies support the use of domain, grid, and payoff-smoothing diagnostics. Far-field truncation affects Black--Scholes PDE error \cite{KangroNicolaides2000}, and nonsmooth payoff data can harm convergence and hedging quantities unless treated carefully \cite{PooleyVetzalForsyth2003,GilesCarter2006}. Recent work on American-style Greeks further supports cautious Delta/Gamma reporting near free boundaries \cite{InTHout2024}.

Neural option-pricing work supports surrogate modeling as an acceleration strategy, not as a replacement for benchmark generation \cite{HutchinsonLoPoggio1994,RufWang2020,LiuOosterleeBohte2019}. Constraint-aware and no-arbitrage neural-network papers motivate hard structural constraints and audits because ordinary networks do not automatically preserve financial shape properties \cite{DugasEtAl2009,YangZhengHospedales2017,CaoLiuZhai2021}.

Derivative-aware and optimal-stopping machine-learning papers motivate modular treatment of price, boundary, and Delta tasks \cite{BeckerCheriditoJentzen2019,HugeSavine2020,FrandsenPedersenPoulsen2022}. Reproducible computational research guidance supports the project's use of configuration manifests, experiment matrices, and failure-regime reporting \cite{SandveEtAl2013,WilsonEtAl2014}.

\section{Mathematical Formulation}
Variational inequality and LCP formulation. Define the payoff, obstacle condition, continuation and exercise regions, complementarity residual, and early-exercise boundary. Emphasize that this formulation is standard and used to organize diagnostics.

\section{Benchmark Method}
Crank--Nicolson, PSOR, boundary extraction, Greeks, and diagnostics. Present CN/PSOR as the audited benchmark and label generator. Include solver validation, grid/domain/runtime sensitivity, obstacle and complementarity checks, and masked Delta/Gamma reporting. The smoke-grid European put and no-dividend American call max errors are approximately $2.78\times10^{-4}$, while American put obstacle violation is $0.0$ and complementarity is near $4.01\times10^{-12}$.

\section{Application Experiments}
Stress-regime risk maps for American puts and dividend-paying American calls. Separate American puts, dividend-paying calls, and no-dividend call controls. Report delicate regimes rather than filtering them out. The application sweep covers 288 regimes and preserves 32 diagnostic-failure or delicate regimes in the audit outputs.

\section{Neural Surrogate Workflow}
Positive-premium price surrogate, direct boundary diagnostic head, bounded supervised Delta head. The positive continuation-premium model should be described as a literature-inspired hard obstacle parameterization, not as an established standard architecture. Boundary and Delta heads should be scoped as separate diagnostic tasks.

Dataset construction uses 3,264,943 samples from 223 clean PSOR regimes with regime-level train/validation/test splits. Direct-price baselines are retained as negative evidence because they do not hard-enforce the obstacle. The selected positive-premium MLP has validation RMSE about $0.0200$ and obstacle violation $0.0$ on evaluated samples. The pooled boundary MLP has test boundary RMSE about $0.3513$ and MAE about $0.2087$, so boundary estimates should be described as scoped diagnostics. The bounded supervised Delta MLP has strict-mask test RMSE about $0.0332$ and zero Delta-bound violation in the evaluated samples.

\section{Results}
Price, boundary, Delta, violation, and runtime comparisons. The integrated workflow combines three separate heads and is audited on six fresh PSOR held-out regimes. The mean price RMSE is about $0.00919$, mean boundary MAE is about $0.1539$, mean strict Delta RMSE is about $0.0300$, and mean speedup is about $87.5$. Obstacle and Delta-bound violations are $0.0$ in the integrated audit.

Claim wording should remain conservative: the workflow supports scenario screening with PSOR audit. It should not be described as a replacement for the benchmark solver, as a Gamma model, or as a single network for all risk quantities.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
PSOR remains the audit standard. Current limits include engineering-scale rather than final publication-grade solver evidence, masked rather than universal Greek stability, weak boundary generalization, and only six fresh integrated audit cases. Extensions include larger regimes, active learning, uncertainty quantification, shape-constrained models, and richer dynamics.
